Things to notice

  • An open question starts with what, how, or when and leaves the answer to the other person. A leading question carries your answer inside it. A "why" question about a person usually lands as blame.
  • The advice trap: the moment you tell her what to do, the plan becomes yours and the commitment becomes hers to resist. Hold your idea until the Options stage, and offer it as one more option.
  • Reality before Options: most coaching fails because the manager jumps to solutions before both people see the same picture.
  • Will is not "good luck": it is a first step, a date, and a named obstacle. Commitment rises when the person says the step out loud.
  • Just culture asks about the behavior, not the outcome: the same near miss can be human error (console), at-risk behavior (coach), or reckless (discipline).
  • Delegation transfers the task, never the accountability. The RN keeps the assessment and the judgment; the follow-up is part of the handover.
