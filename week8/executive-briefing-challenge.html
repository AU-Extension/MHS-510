Week 8 · Challenge

Executive briefing challenge

You are presenting the 4 West improvement proposal (two night RN positions on the weekend line, a fixed charge rotation, and recognition funded from the underspent lines) to five executives. Ten questions and four charts: choose the strongest answer and the headline that states what each chart shows. Score out of 100.

How to use this interactive
  1. Each round an executive asks a question (6 points, 3 for a partial answer) or a chart appears and you pick its headline (10 points, 5 for a headline that describes without insight).
  2. The floor shows who is asking; your answer flies to them in green, amber, or coral.
  3. Read the feedback: it says why that executive cares and cites the file behind the number.
  4. After 14 rounds you get a score out of 100 and can try again with shuffled rounds and answers.
  5. Use the executive checklist at the bottom to rehearse your own final presentation.

The executive checklist for your final presentation

ExecutiveWhat they listen forWhat a weak answer sounds likeWhat a strong answer contains
Chief Nursing OfficerWill it stick, and does it fit the Magnet journey (shared governance, nurse-led change)?"I will make sure it happens."A coalition with names, a pilot, a 90-day win, and the structure that carries it after you.
Chief Financial OfficerCost, offset, payback, and what happens to the overtime and agency lines."It will pay for itself."FTE math ($41 x 2,080 x 1.28), the hours replaced and their rate, the net per year, and the resignation cost avoided.
Chief Medical OfficerPatient harm and the clinical evidence behind the claim."Everyone knows nights are worse."Falls by shift and day of week, the ratio line at 6.5, and an honest word about correlation.
Human Resources directorRecruiting feasibility, retention, fairness, and labor rules."We will pay more."Exit reasons from the file, tenure at exit, a fair rule for the weekend line, and the coaching plan for the charge nurse.
Quality directorMeasures, targets, dates, and the review cadence."Fewer falls."Two or four measures with a baseline, a target, a date, and who reviews them monthly.

Things to notice

  • Executives ask the same five questions in every hospital: what does it cost, what does it save, how do you know, what is the measure, and why will it last. Answer them before they are asked.
  • A headline states the insight with the number; a caption only names the axes. Every chart in your deck needs a headline.
  • The strongest answers cite a file and a row: "schedule.csv, September, 294 overtime hours" beats "a lot of overtime".
  • Naming a risk and a stop rule makes you more credible, not less. Executives have seen the plan with no risks before.
  • Correlation said out loud is a strength. "This pattern is why we test it on a pilot line with monthly review" is what a quality director wants to hear.
