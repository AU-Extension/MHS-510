How this challenge works

  1. Read the scene in the round card below. On the board above, the people of the round walk to the room where it happens (amber), and the claim at stake flies in as a coral packet.
  2. Under 1. What is at work here?, press the bias or ethical principle that fits. The best answer earns 6 points; a defensible second choice earns 2.
  3. Read the feedback. Then, under 2. What is the move?, press the move that fixes it. The best move earns 4 points; a partial move earns 1.
  4. Read why, and watch the move fly to the room on the board above: green fixed, amber partly fixed, coral not fixed. Press Next round.
  5. After round 10, press See my score. Review your points for each round, then press Try again (shuffled) to play with a new order.

Things to notice

  • Most biases are fixed by the same habit: go to the file before you go to the meeting. The scorecard, the incident file and the schedule are the outside view.
  • Groupthink is quiet. A fast, unanimous decision in a room with a strong voice is a warning, not a success.
  • Sunk cost and escalation of commitment look like loyalty. Decide on what the next dollar and the next month will buy, never on what was already spent.
  • An AI assistant writes fluent numbers. Fluency is not evidence: every figure in a report is traced to its file and row before it leaves the unit.
  • The four principles (autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, justice) apply to staff decisions as much as to patient care. A schedule can be unjust; a staffing plan can do harm.
