Things to notice

  • The same census with one fewer RN moves a night from "within target" to "above 6.5", and 70% of the unit's falls happened on the 14% of shifts above that line.
  • Hiring is not one lever but two: the new hours can replace overtime and agency shifts (same coverage, lower cost and less fatigue) or add coverage where the ratio is worst (better nights, no offset). Say which one your proposal means.
  • Cutting overtime without replacing the hours saves money on paper and worsens the ratio on the floor. The scorecard would show it in falls and complaints within a quarter.
  • The HCAHPS and falls predictions come from a 12-month correlation with overtime. They describe the unit's own pattern; they do not prove cause, and a proposal should say so.
  • One resignation costs more than half of a new RN's yearly pay before a single vacant shift is covered.
How to use this interactive
  1. Pick a shift and a day of the week. The board loads that shift's averages from six months of daily_census.csv.
  2. Move one lever at a time (census, hires, overtime, agency, target) and read the board and the formula box.
  3. Press Explain to hear the seven steps narrated on the floor, or Step through them.
  4. Click a nurse token to see the rooms she covers; coral means her load is above 6.5 patients.
  5. Use the resignation calculator and the scorecard table, then take the six-question self-check.
Where every number comes from
  • Shift averages: daily_census.csv, 2026-04-01 to 2026-09-30, one row per date and shift (366 rows), grouped by shift and day of week.
  • Overtime, agency and float shifts per shift type: schedule.csv, the 4 weeks of September 2026 (597 rows).
  • Falls per 100 shifts: incidents.csv falls joined to the census rows by date and shift: 19 falls on the 52 shifts above 6.5 patients per RN, 8 on the other 314.
  • Predicted HCAHPS and falls per 1,000: a least-squares line through the 12 scorecard months against overtime percent (r squared 0.82 and 0.71).
  • Pay and cost facts: the data README. RN base $41 an hour, 2,080 paid hours per 1.0 FTE, 28% benefits, overtime 1.5 x base, agency $95 an hour, $62,000 to recruit and onboard one RN.
