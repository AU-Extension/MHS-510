    Who holds which power?

    French and Raven named six bases of power: legitimate (the position), reward (what you can give), coercive (what you can take away or punish), expert (what you know how to do), referent (people want to be like you or be liked by you), and informational (what you know that others need). Pick a person, mark the bases you think they hold on 4 West, and check.

    Influence tactics the manager can pair with each base

    • Rational persuasion (facts and logic): pairs with informational and expert power. The 11 reports and 3 exits are the facts.
    • Consultation (asking the person to help shape the plan): pairs with referent and legitimate power. "How would you build the night check-in?"
    • Coalition (bringing in others who agree): pairs with referent power. Yesenia, Beatriz, and Dalia on the same norm across shifts.
    • Pressure (demands, deadlines, reminders): pairs with legitimate and coercive power. Kept in reserve, and documented when used.
    • Exchange (offering something in return): pairs with reward power. A coaching role, certification support from the $38,800 unspent education budget.

    Things to notice

    • The standard (no public correction of a nurse) is the same in every mode. What changes is whether the manager states it, listens, and builds the "how" with Kwame.
    • Collaborating costs the most minutes this week and the least over the year: one night RN resignation costs about $62,000 to recruit and onboard, before the agency hours to cover the vacancy.
    • The comments and reports never name Kwame. The schedule shows him as charge nurse on the night of INC-096 and Yesenia on the night of INC-082. Talk to people before you conclude (Week 2).
    • Power is not only the manager's. Naomi's informational power (what happens at 1 a.m.) is worth more than any memo, and it only flows when speaking up is safe.
