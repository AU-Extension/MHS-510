    1. Replay the incident through four pairs of eyes

    Perception is the process of selecting, organizing, and interpreting what we notice. Each person noticed a different slice of the afternoon, and each filled the gaps with an assumption. Attribution is the cause we assign to a behavior: inside the person (internal) or in the situation (external).

    Things to notice

    • Nobody in the replay lied. Each person saw a real slice of the afternoon and filled the rest with an assumption. That is the perceptual process doing what it always does.
    • The fundamental attribution error explains other people's behavior by who they are; the self-serving bias explains our own failures by the situation. Camila and Daniel made opposite errors about the same dose.
    • The first report a manager hears becomes the frame. Reading the record before forming a conclusion is the cheapest de-biasing tool there is.
    • Consistency, distinctiveness, and consensus are three questions, not a verdict. When one cue is missing, the right move is to gather it, not to guess.
    • Eleven of the eighteen medication errors on 4 West in six months involved an RN on an overtime shift. One careless nurse is a story; eleven of eighteen is a system.
