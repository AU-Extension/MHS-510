How to use this explorer

  1. Press ▶ Play under the board, or Step ▶, to see what the team does in this stage. Read the narration in the box under the board. The stage names run across the top of The leader's move card below.
  2. Click one of the four moves in The leader's move card below. Feedback appears under the moves: green is the best fit for the stage, coral is a weaker move, and the best move is named.
  3. Watch the two meters on the board above, psychological safety and productivity (0 to 10). They rise or fall with your move, and the box under the board explains why. Then press Next stage ▶.
  4. Repeat steps 1 to 3 for all five stages. After adjourning, press See the summary ▶: it counts how many of your five moves were the best fit. Start over lets you try other moves.
  5. Click the tab 2 · One handoff, with and without SBAR above the board. Press ▶ Play for Without SBAR, then click With SBAR and play it. Compare the two reports in the table below.
  6. Answer the six questions in the Self-check at the bottom of the page. Each answer tells you why it is right or wrong.

Tuckman's five stages and the leader's job

StageWhat you see on the teamWhat the leader doesWhat psychological safety needs
FormingPolite, cautious, dependent on the leader; people size each other upGive purpose, roles, and ground rules; make sure every voice is heard onceAn invitation to speak and a leader who admits what she does not know
StormingOpen disagreement about the problem, the method, or who decides; productivity dipsName conflict as useful, bring the data, keep it about the issue, not the personDisagreement answered with curiosity, never with rank
NormingAgreement on how to work; roles settle; trust startsLet the team write its norms and decide how it decidesNorms the team owns, including how to raise a concern
PerformingThe team solves problems on its own and runs its own huddlesStep back, remove obstacles, provide resources, recognize results by nameMistakes treated as information, not blame
AdjourningThe work ends; relief and loss at the same timeDebrief, record what to keep, thank each person, report the resultA closing that says the risk people took was worth it

Bruce Tuckman described forming, storming, norming, and performing in 1965; Tuckman and Mary Ann Jensen added adjourning in 1977. Amy Edmondson defines psychological safety as a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk taking. A change in membership (a new member, a lost member) sends a team back toward forming.
