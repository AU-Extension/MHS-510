    Things to notice

    • Skipping step 2 costs more than skipping any other step: Dalia (influence 5) stays at -2, Carmen follows her, and the pilot has no day-shift owner. Watch the "will it stick" number.
    • Rushing every step gives a run that looks fine on the floor (the champions turn green) and still fails, because nobody who was against the change moved.
    • Steps 7 and 8 do not move many dots, and they carry the most weight in the score. Last year's attempt did steps 1 to 5 and stopped: it faded in three weeks, which is comment C015.
    • The first three Kotter steps are Lewin's unfreeze; steps 5 to 7 are the change; step 8 is refreeze. Schein's deepest level, the assumption that "new ideas fade, so wait them out", is what step 8 has to replace.
    • The change costs almost nothing in money: the whole budget is printed sheets and a two-hour educator session. What it costs is attention, for 90 days.
    How to use this interactive
    1. Read the four urgency facts. They come from the course data files.
    2. Leave every step on "Done well" and press Play. Watch the tokens change color and the "will it stick" number climb.
    3. Set step 2 to "Skipped" and play again. Then try rushing every step. Compare the final numbers.
    4. Open the stakeholder map, pick the four most influential people, and plan where you want each one in 90 days. Read the suggested move for each quadrant.
    5. Finish with the six self-check questions, then use your map in Assignment 6.2.
