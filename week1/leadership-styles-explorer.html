    Things to notice

    • Run the same style for the new nurse and for the charge nurse. The moves are identical; the meters are not. That is the whole point of situational leadership: the follower's readiness decides the style, not the manager's preference.
    • Telling (S1) gives the best task result for Naomi tonight and the worst trust result for Yesenia. Delegating (S4) does the reverse.
    • Transformational and servant leadership raise trust and learning for both followers, but neither replaces the "who do I see first" that a new nurse needs at 7 p.m. They work on top of the situational choice, over months, not minutes.
    • Stress drops when people know what is expected and feel backed. It rises when they are left guessing, whatever the style is called.
    • Leadership moves are violet on the floor; the manager's own tasks (the call to staffing, the walk to 418) are the management side of the same job.
    How to use this interactive
    1. Read the hour you inherit: the census, the call-out, the ratio, and the family at room 418.
    2. Pick one of the six leadership styles and choose who you lead tonight: Naomi, a nurse in her first year, or Yesenia, the night charge nurse.
    3. Press Run, or Step through the six moments of the hour. Watch the tokens move on the floor and the four meters respond; the narration names the theory behind each move.
    4. Switch the follower and run the same style again. Then try the other styles.
    5. Answer the six self-check questions at the bottom.
